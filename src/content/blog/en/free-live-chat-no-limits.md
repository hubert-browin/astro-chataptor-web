Free live chat sounds simple. You add a chat widget to your website, visitors can ask questions, and the team replies faster than through a contact form.

The important question is what "free" actually means. Sometimes only the trial is free. Sometimes the free plan limits conversations, users or features. Sometimes the widget is useful, but the team later needs mobile support, email, social media, e-commerce messages or automatic translations.

This guide compares tawk.to, Smartsupp, Tidio and Chataptor in a practical way. Pricing, limits and feature packaging can change, so always check the provider's current information before choosing a tool.

## What companies really want from free live chat

Most teams are not looking for a chat icon only. They want to solve a real communication problem:

- visitors leave the website without asking a question,
- contact forms are too slow,
- replies arrive too late,
- messages are scattered across several apps,
- the owner or sales team needs to answer from a phone,
- an online store needs faster pre-sale support,
- international customers write in different languages,
- the company wants to test chat without signing a long contract.

The first question is usually: "Can we add chat to the website for free?" Later, the better questions appear: are there message limits, can we use a mobile app, can the team work together, can we connect email and social channels, and can we handle foreign customers without copying everything into a translator?

## What to check before choosing a free chat widget

First, check message or conversation limits. A free plan with limits can be enough for a small website, but it can become uncomfortable exactly when chat starts working and customers send more messages.

Second, check branding. Some companies do not care about "powered by" labels. Others, especially B2B, premium stores, hotels, clinics or exporters, may want a cleaner customer-facing experience.

Third, check mobile support. Live chat only works when someone answers. If new messages are visible only in a desktop panel, the team may miss them while away from the office. We cover this workflow in the guide to [customer support from a phone](/en/blog/customer-support-from-phone/).

Fourth, check channels. Customers do not always write through the website chat. They may use email, Messenger, WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook or marketplaces. That is why many teams need an [omnichannel inbox](/en/blog/omnichannel-inbox-customer-messages/), not just one widget.
